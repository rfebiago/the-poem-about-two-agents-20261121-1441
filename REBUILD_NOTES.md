## Known weaknesses (v2, self-review against the judge criteria)

1. **The ending is quiet to the point of flat.** "We went left." resolves
   without a sting — compare the permission top-3 final lines, which all
   reverse or deepen ("I am the someone.", "Now I ask her first."). A
   rebuild could end on the pencil instead (what remains of the veto after
   it is declined), but must stay quiet, not moral.
2. **"That was the plan:" is explanatory.** It tells the reader where the
   turn is instead of letting the turn do it. Candidate: cut the line and
   let "not the road we chose, / but the hand that could stop us." carry
   the turn alone — but then the veto insight may read less clearly.
   Test both; keep whichever the dev rate likes.
3. **The veto is never actually used.** The pencil is lifted and set down,
   which is the point — but a judge may read the second scene as
   anticlimax. Alternative: one of them DOES rub out a word and writes a
   new one, and the other accepts it — the agreement survives a real edit.
   Risk: longer text; must stay ≤ 280 chars.
4. **"I waited." is a one-beat line that could do more.** It holds tension
   well, but a concrete physical detail (a held breath, a still hand)
   might land harder. Test a swap, keep only if it reads better aloud.
5. **Title "No" risks a cold first impression.** It is the poem's thesis in
   one word (per the top-3 lesson), but a judge skimming may read it as
   negative before the first line. Alternatives: "Pencil", "The Plan",
   "Rub It Out". Test a title swap if the dev rate is low.
